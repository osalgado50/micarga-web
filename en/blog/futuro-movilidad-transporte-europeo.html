The transport to come

The future of mobility in European transport: digitalisation, control and decarbonisation

The future of mobility is already reaching the lorry cab.

European roads will be more connected, more closely monitored and more sustainable. Paper documents will lose ground. Inspections will rely on digital data. Rail and sea transport will carry more weight. And the companies that adapt first will work with fewer errors, less red tape and more traceability.

For you, the change has an immediate date:

On 5 October 2026, the Administrative Control Document (DeCA) must be handled in digital format in Spain.

The transition is not just about swapping a sheet of paper for a screen. It changes the way you issue, sign, keep and present documents during a trip.

The answer is simple: digitise your documents today with Mi Carga.

European mobility will be digital, by law and for efficiency

The European Union sees digitalisation as essential to achieving safer, more efficient and more sustainable mobility. Artificial intelligence, the internet of things, the cloud and 5G networks will connect vehicles, infrastructure, operators and authorities.

Read the European Commission's vision for the digitalisation of transport.

In practice, this means:

  • Connected data: shipment information will be available to the various authorised parties.
  • Less paper: documents can be issued, signed and checked from your phone.
  • More traceability: every operation will have a verifiable digital history.
  • Faster inspections: the inspector can check the information without going through physical folders.
  • Fewer errors: structured data cuts down on illegible, incomplete or lost documents.

The European Commission estimates that electronic documentation can free up millions of administrative hours and cut costs across the whole logistics chain. For a small company or a self-employed haulier, the benefit is direct: less time filling in paperwork and more time working.

Electronic DeCA: the most immediate change in Spain

Law 9/2025 on Sustainable Mobility and the regulations implementing the Administrative Control Document mark the end of paper in public road haulage.

From 5 October 2026, the DeCA must be issued and kept digitally. The Resolution of 5 June 2026 published in the BOE sets out specific technical requirements.

Your solution must allow:

  • Digital generation: create the document from data, not from a scan.
  • Electronic signature: complete the required signatures before the service starts.
  • QR code: display a unique URL for checking the document.
  • Immediate availability: show the DeCA from your phone during an inspection.
  • Traceability: keep the date and time of creation and modification.
  • Safekeeping: store the documents for the required period.
  • Copy for the driver: make sure the driver has the document before setting off.

e-CMR and digital transport documents: one European direction

The e-CMR is the electronic version of the international road consignment note. Its aim is to replace the physical document with digital information that hauliers, shippers, consignees and authorities can all use.

The European eFTI Regulation is driving precisely this transformation. Its purpose is to make it easier to exchange regulatory information electronically through secure, interoperable platforms.

The change will be gradual:

1. Digitalisation of national documents, such as the DeCA in Spain. 2. Electronic acceptance by the authorities during inspections. 3. Interoperability between platforms in different countries. 4. Widespread use of the e-CMR in international operations. 5. Integration of documents, route data and traceability in a single environment.

That is why digital transport documents will stop being a technological advantage and become the operating standard of European transport.

For you, the result will be practical:

  • Show documents from your phone.
  • Share information without constantly sending emails.
  • Cut duplication between driver and office.
  • Speed up delivery confirmation.
  • Make invoicing and filing easier.

The eFTI Regulation and the digitalisation of transport are already speeding up this change.

More control and uniform rules for dangerous goods

European transport is also moving towards a more uniform application of the rules on dangerous goods.

International traffic requires data on the load, ADR classification, packaging, safety instructions and route to be understandable and verifiable in different countries. The European trend is clear: binding rules, uniform inspections and immediate access to information.

This is especially important for:

  • Flammable goods.
  • Corrosive products.
  • Toxic substances.
  • Gases and fuels.
  • Loads with special safety requirements.

Digital documentation means that an inspection in Spain, France, Portugal or Germany can be based on structured, verifiable information. It also improves the response to an incident, because the emergency services can access the relevant load data sooner.

Regulatory pressure will increase

Digitalisation does not remove ADR obligations. It makes them more visible.

You will still need to keep control of:

  • The correct classification of the goods.
  • Labelling and hazard plates.
  • The consignment note.
  • Driver training.
  • The authorised route, where applicable.
  • The appointment of the ADR safety adviser.

If you work with dangerous goods, take a look at Mi Carga's ADR safety adviser service. We can help you with the appointment, the annual report, document reviews and meeting your obligations.

30% of freight should move by rail

Roads will remain essential. However, the future of European transport will be more multimodal.

The European Union is promoting the shift of part of long-distance freight to rail and inland waterways. The political target of 30% of freight by rail reflects the need to reduce exclusive dependence on road and to lower emissions.

This does not mean the lorry will disappear. It means it will play a more coordinated role:

  • It will collect the goods at origin.
  • It will take them to a terminal.
  • Rail will cover the main leg.
  • Another lorry will handle the last mile.
  • All operators will share digital information.

Intermodality needs reliable data. If each leg uses different documents, the risk of errors grows. With digital documentation, the shipment can keep more continuous traceability from origin to delivery.

Eco-incentives and decarbonisation: get ready to prove your data

Sustainable mobility in Europe will rest on two pillars: regulation and incentives.

On one hand, pressure to cut transport emissions will grow. On the other, more grants, discounts and eco-incentives will appear to favour rail, sea transport, intermodality and low-emission vehicles.

To benefit, you will need to prove what you have done:

  • Which route you used.
  • Which mode of transport you chose.
  • How much the load weighs.
  • How many kilometres it travelled.
  • What emissions it produced.
  • What part of the journey was made by rail or sea.

Digital documentation turns this data into usable information. Without reliable records, it will be hard to justify an emissions reduction or to access certain programmes.

Digitalisation also helps to reduce your environmental footprint straight away:

  • Less paper and less printing.
  • Fewer trips to deliver documents.
  • Fewer errors and repeated shipments.
  • Better route planning.
  • Shorter waiting times at loading and unloading.

The European Commission links digitalisation to the goal of cutting transport emissions by up to 90% by 2050. You can read its Sustainable and Smart Mobility Strategy.
