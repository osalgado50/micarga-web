Setting up and running the business

Requirements to set up a goods transport company in Spain

Setting up a goods transport company in Spain takes much more than buying a truck or a van. You need to choose a legal form, meet the transport requirements, prove professional and financial capacity, take out the right insurance and keep your tax obligations up to date.

The good news is that you can organise it step by step and start with a single vehicle. In this guide you'll find the main requirements to set up a road haulage company in 2026.

Important: the procedures may vary depending on the type of vehicle, whether you operate nationally or internationally, and the autonomous community where you establish the company. Before starting the activity, check your specific case with the competent Administration.

1. Choose how you'll work: self-employed or company

The first step is to decide the legal form of your business.

Working as a self-employed person (autónomo)

It's the simplest option if you're starting alone and will drive your own vehicle.

You'll need to:

  • Register with the Tax Agency (Hacienda) using form 036 or 037.
  • Choose the IAE heading that corresponds to the transport activity.
  • Register with the RETA, the Special Scheme for Self-Employed Workers.
  • Declare your tax address and the activity you'll carry out.
  • Meet your VAT (IVA), IRPF and invoicing obligations.

As a self-employed person, you can start with a lean structure and control the activity directly. You'll also personally bear the financial liability of the business.

Setting up a company

You can incorporate a Sociedad Limitada (S.L.) if you're going to work with several partners, hire drivers or manage a fleet.

The company will need:

  • A company name.
  • A public deed of incorporation.
  • The company's NIF.
  • Registration with the Tax Agency.
  • Registration in the Commercial Registry.
  • A corporate purpose that includes the transport of goods.
  • Its own bank account and accounting.

The S.L. offers greater separation between personal and business assets, although it involves more paperwork, management costs and accounting obligations.

Practical tip: if you're starting alone with a van or a truck, consider going self-employed. If you want to build a fleet, hire staff or bring in partners, look into a company from the start.

2. Get your haulier qualification

One of the core requirements to set up a goods transport company is to prove professional competence in transport.

This requirement is usually known as:

  • Haulier qualification (título de transportista).
  • Certificate of professional competence.
  • Professional qualification.
  • Transport manager.

The company must have a person who holds this qualification and who actually carries out the transport management duties.

Among other tasks, the manager must oversee:

  • How services are contracted.
  • Vehicle maintenance.
  • Transport documentation.
  • Route planning.
  • Compliance with driving and rest times.
  • Handling incidents and penalties.

If you're self-employed, you can be the transport manager yourself if you hold the certificate. If you set up a company, the person performing this role must have a real and effective relationship with the company, under the conditions required by the regulations.

Without this requirement, you won't be able to operate properly under the authorisations that require it.

Start here: check whether you need to obtain the certificate, hire a transport manager or prove the qualification through a person linked to your company.

3. Meet the good repute requirement

Good repute is another necessary condition to work as a haulier.

The Administration may check that the company and the transport manager have not committed infringements or offences that prevent them from carrying out the activity.

You can lose good repute for reasons such as:

  • Very serious transport infringements.
  • Repeated breaches of driving and rest times.
  • Tachograph tampering.
  • Transport without authorisation.
  • Serious breaches related to road safety.
  • Offences related to the professional activity.

Good repute isn't a document you have to apply for every year. It's a condition you must maintain throughout your activity.

Practical result: keeping orderly document control helps you spot errors before an inspection and protect the continuity of your company.

4. Prove your financial capacity

The regulations require you to prove that you have enough resources to maintain the vehicles and carry out the activity.

For transport vehicles subject to the full regime, the usual reference is:

€9,000 for the first vehicle

€5,000 for each additional vehicle

This amount can be proven through capital, reserves, a bank guarantee or other means accepted by the Administration.

Example:

  • 1 vehicle: €9,000
  • 2 vehicles: €14,000
  • 3 vehicles: €19,000

Don't confuse the financial capacity of a haulage company with the requirements for an agency, transport operator or intermediary company. Each authorisation may have different conditions.

If you're going to work with light vehicles, especially between 2.5 and 3.5 tonnes in international transport, check the applicable regime carefully. Certain international services require conditions similar to those for heavy vehicles.

Before buying the vehicle, confirm the amount that applies to you. That way you'll avoid investing in a unit you then can't assign to the authorisation.

5. Buy or lease the right vehicle

You don't need a minimum fleet of several trucks to get started. In many cases you can apply for the authorisation with a single vehicle, as long as you meet the required conditions.

The vehicle can be:

  • Owned.
  • Under finance lease.
  • Rented, when the applicable regime allows it.

It must also meet the corresponding technical and administrative conditions:

  • A valid roadworthiness test (ITV).
  • Valid technical data sheet.
  • Correct registration and documentation.
  • Compulsory insurance.
  • Suitability for the load you'll carry.
  • Safety equipment.
  • Documented maintenance.

If you're going to work in urban areas or carry out international transport, also check environmental restrictions and low-emission zones.

A wrong purchase decision can compromise your profitability from day one. Choose the vehicle according to the load, the routes, the MMA (maximum authorised mass) and the type of authorisation you need.

6. Apply for the transport card

To carry out public road haulage you need the corresponding administrative authorisation. This authorisation is linked to the vehicles and is reflected in what is known as the transport card (tarjeta de transporte).

The best known are:

MDL authorisation

Generally used for light vehicles intended for hire-and-reward goods transport.

MDP authorisation

Applies to heavy vehicles for public goods transport, normally those exceeding 3.5 tonnes MMA.

The authorisation requires meeting requirements regarding establishment, documentation and vehicles and, depending on the case, professional competence, good repute and financial capacity.

Information and procedures can be found on the Ministry of Transport's frequently asked questions page and in your autonomous community's procedures, such as the Community of Madrid's information on MDL and MDP authorisations.

Don't start hauling for third parties without authorisation. Working without the corresponding card can lead to penalties, immobilisation of the vehicle and problems with your customers.

7. Register in the register of haulage companies

Once the authorisation has been granted, the company must be correctly listed in the Register of Transport Companies and Activities.

This register makes it possible to identify:

  • The holder of the activity.
  • The authorised vehicles.
  • The type of transport.
  • The company's address.
  • The transport manager.
  • The administrative status of the authorisation.

Keep the company's details up to date. If you change address, vehicle, manager or legal form, check whether you need to report it.

8. Take out compulsory and recommended insurance

The main compulsory insurance is motor third-party liability insurance for each vehicle.

In addition, to work safely and be accountable to your customers, it's usually advisable to take out:

  • Haulier's liability insurance.
  • Goods insurance or cover for damage to the load.
  • General liability insurance for the company.
  • Accident insurance for drivers.
  • Roadside assistance.
  • Legal cover.

The policy must be adapted to the type of goods, routes, liability limits and the terms of your contracts.

Don't choose on price alone. Insufficient cover can lead to significant losses if the goods are damaged, lost or cause damage to third parties.

9. Meet your tax and employment obligations

Once the company is up and running, you'll have to keep on top of its management.

Tax obligations

Depending on whether you're self-employed or a company, you'll have to handle:

  • Quarterly and annual VAT (IVA).
  • IRPF, if you work as self-employed.
  • Corporate Income Tax, if you have an S.L.
  • Invoicing and accounting books.
  • Withholdings for employees or professionals.
  • Informative returns.
  • Instalment payments.

Employment obligations

If you hire drivers, you'll have to comply with:

  • Registration with Social Security.
  • Employment contracts.
  • Payroll and contributions.
  • Occupational risk prevention.
  • Applicable collective agreement.
  • CAP training.
  • Driving times, breaks and rest periods.
  • Managing holidays and sick leave.

Transport documentation

Organise and keep properly:

  • Control documents.
  • Consignment notes.
  • Contracts and loading orders.
  • Proofs of delivery.
  • Tachograph records.
  • Vehicle documentation.
  • Invoices and delivery notes.

10. Steps to set up your transport company

This is the practical order you can follow:

1. Choose self-employed or company. 2. Register with the Tax Agency and Social Security. 3. Prove professional competence. 4. Check your good repute. 5. Prove financial capacity. 6. Buy or lease the right vehicle. 7. Take out compulsory insurance and the cover you need. 8. Apply for the MDL or MDP card. 9. Check your entry in the register of haulage companies. 10. Organise taxes, documentation and day-to-day management. You don't need to sort everything out at once. Start by defining the type of transport, the vehicle and the routes you'll run. Then apply for the authorisation that matches your activity.

How much does it cost to set up a transport company?

The budget depends on several factors:

  • Legal form.
  • Vehicle price.
  • Down payment or leasing instalments.
  • Required financial capacity.
  • Insurance.
  • Accountancy firm (gestoría).
  • Fuel.
  • Maintenance.
  • Authorisations.
  • Salaries, if you hire drivers.
  • Documentation and control systems.

The vehicle is usually the biggest investment, but it's not the only cost. Also calculate the fixed costs for the first few months so you don't run out of cash before you get paid for your first jobs.
