Digital DeCA: why paper stops counting on 5 October 2026

A guide for hauliers and road transport companies operating in Spain.

If you carry goods domestically for hire and reward in Spain, there is a date to circle: 5 October 2026. From that day, the administrative control document must exist in digital form. Paper on its own will no longer be valid.

The answer is called the DeCA: the electronic Administrative Control Document. You can start using it today with Mi Carga, straight from your phone, with 10 free trial consignments.

The change is already law

The obligation comes from Act 9/2025 of 3 December on Sustainable Mobility, whose eighth transitional provision states that the control document must be digital ten months after the Act comes into force: 5 October 2026.

The Resolution of 5 June 2026 sets out the technical requirements that the documents, and the software used to produce them, must meet. A hand-filled sheet is no longer enough, nor is photographing it or scanning it into a PDF. From that date, the document must be:

  • Digital: produced directly from an application.
  • Traceable: with the date and time it was created and changed.
  • Accessible: available for a roadside inspection.
  • Verifiable: with a QR code and a direct URL.
  • Retained: for at least 12 months.

What exactly is the digital DeCA?

The DeCA is the electronic version of the administrative control document that records the main details of the job: who loads, who carries, what goods are being moved and where they come from and go to. What changes is not its purpose but its format and the way it is validated.

At a roadside check the driver can show it on a phone or present a printed copy, as long as that copy comes from the digital document and carries its QR code. Paper doesn't disappear altogether: what stops being valid is a document created only on paper.

Which journeys does it apply to?

The digital DeCA will be mandatory for domestic carriage of goods for hire and reward — origin and destination within Spain — and for cabotage operations carried out on Spanish territory.

Outside its scope are, among others: international transport (documented with a CMR or eCMR), regulated removals, breakdown or accident recovery movements, and transport exempted under Order FOM/2861/2012.

If you carry dangerous goods, the DeCA does not replace ADR paperwork: they are separate obligations that sit side by side. You can produce both from the same place.
