Setting up and running the business

Becoming a self-employed haulier in Spain: requirements, paperwork and obligations in 2026

Looking into how to become a self-employed haulier? Want to know what self-employed hauliers need to work legally in Spain?

In 2026 it is not enough to buy a van or a lorry and start invoicing. You have tax, employment and administrative obligations to meet. You also need to keep your paperwork under control before every job.

The good news is that you can organise it simply. Getting the admin right beforehand avoids mistakes, delays and fines. In fact, up to 40% of the fines that hit the transport sector can be avoided by checking the paperwork before setting off.

In this guide you will find the steps to register, apply for the transport licence, meet the CAP requirements and work as a genuinely self-employed haulier, without falling into bogus self-employment.

What do you need to become a self-employed haulier in 2026?

To start a road haulage business you need to prepare the following:

  • Tax registration: register your activity with the Spanish Tax Agency (Hacienda) under the right heading, usually 722, which covers road haulage.
  • RETA registration: sign up as a self-employed worker with Social Security before you start trading.
  • Suitable vehicle: it must be in your name, on leasing, renting or another arrangement that lets you legally use it.
  • Transport licence: apply for the authorisation that matches the type of vehicle and the service you will provide.
  • Valid CAP: if you drive vehicles professionally that require it, you must hold the Certificate of Professional Competence (CAP).
  • Compulsory insurance: keep your vehicle insurance up to date and consider goods-in-transit and haulier's liability cover.
  • Good repute: keep an administrative record that is compatible with holding on to your authorisation.
  • Paperwork for every job: carry the information you need to justify the transport at a roadside check.

Start by defining your activity, the type of vehicle and whether you will work in domestic or international haulage. That decision shapes much of your paperwork.

1. Register with Hacienda and Social Security

The first step to becoming a self-employed haulier is to register your activity properly.

Registering with Hacienda

You must file the relevant census form and choose the right heading of the Tax on Economic Activities (IAE). Road haulage usually uses heading 722, although you should confirm your case based on the services you will provide.

You will also need to choose your tax obligations:

  • VAT (IVA): periodic returns and an annual summary, where applicable.
  • Income tax (IRPF): instalment payments or withholdings, depending on how you invoice.
  • Invoicing: issue complete, numbered invoices with all the mandatory details.
  • Deductible expenses: fuel, maintenance, insurance, tolls, accountancy fees and other business-related costs.

Registering with RETA

Next you must register with the Special Scheme for Self-Employed Workers (RETA). Report your activity, your expected earnings and the contribution base you choose within the corresponding bracket.

Do not start working before completing these registrations. If you invoice without being properly registered, you may face back assessments, surcharges and problems during an inspection.

See also the official Social Security information for self-employed workers.

2. Apply for the transport licence you need

The transport licence (tarjeta de transporte) is the administrative authorisation that allows you to carry out public road haulage for third parties. The type of authorisation depends on factors such as:

  • The vehicle's GVW (MMA).
  • Whether you do domestic or international haulage.
  • The type of goods.
  • Ownership or availability of the vehicle.
  • The structure of your business and its administrative requirements.

Generally, you will see references to MDL authorisations for light vehicles and MDP for heavy haulage. However, you should not choose one by its name alone. Check your specific case with the competent authority.

To apply, you will normally have to prove:

  • Establishment in Spain.
  • A suitable vehicle, owned, leased or rented.
  • Valid ITV (roadworthiness test).
  • Compulsory insurance.
  • Tax and Social Security registration.
  • Compliance with tax and social security obligations.
  • Good repute.
  • Professional competence and financial standing, where required.

If you do international haulage with vehicles over 2.5 tonnes and up to 3.5 tonnes, pay particular attention to the additional requirements of EU rules.

Check the up-to-date conditions in the Spanish Ministry of Transport's official information on goods transport.

3. Get and keep your CAP

The CAP is a personal requirement for professional drivers. It does not replace the transport licence, nor does it on its own prove that you can operate as a haulage business.

If you are going to drive a lorry professionally, you will normally need:

  • The right driving licence, such as C or C+E.
  • Initial CAP, to get started under the corresponding route.
  • Periodic training, to keep your qualification.
  • Valid CAP, shown on your documents or licence where applicable.

Keep an eye on the expiry date. Working with an expired CAP can lead to a fine and leave you unable to provide the service legally.

As well as the CAP, check before every route:

  • Driving licence.
  • Transport licence.
  • Compulsory insurance.
  • ITV.
  • Control document or consignment note.
  • Goods documentation.
  • Specific authorisations, if you carry dangerous or special goods.

Keep everything in paper or digital form. Having your paperwork available instantly cuts the time lost at checks and saves you from relying on files scattered around your phone.

4. Keep your good repute to hold on to your authorisation

Good repute is one of the requirements for obtaining and keeping certain transport authorisations.

It is not a document you can keep in the glovebox. It is a condition tied to your legal and administrative conduct. Serious or very serious infringements relating to transport, road safety, driving times, rest periods or social legislation can affect your authorisation.

Losing good repute can have direct consequences:

  • Suspension of the authorisation.
  • Loss of the transport licence.
  • Temporary inability to operate.
  • Financial losses from vehicle immobilisation or cancelled jobs.

That is why you should review any incidents in your business and act before they pile up. Law 16/1987 on Land Transport (LOTT) and its implementing regulations set out the general framework.

5. How much does a self-employed haulier pay in 2026?

The self-employed contribution in 2026 is not the same for everyone. It is calculated on expected net earnings, that is, income minus applicable deductible expenses.

Social Security uses different brackets, from earnings below 670 euros a month to earnings above 6,000 euros a month.

Bear these points in mind:

  • Variable contribution: it depends on your earnings bracket.
  • Choice of base: within the limits set for your bracket.
  • Adjustment: if your actual earnings are higher or lower than expected, the contribution may be adjusted.
  • Reduced contribution: if it is your first registration or you meet the requirements, you can apply for the reduction available to new self-employed workers.
  • Benefits: the base you choose affects benefits such as temporary incapacity, cessation of activity or retirement.

Use the official RETA 2026 contribution simulator before you start trading. Enter your estimated net earnings and check the approximate amount.

Do not work out your profitability from the contribution alone. Also include fuel, tyres, maintenance, insurance, accountancy, tolls, taxes, financing and periods without loads.

6. Avoid being classed as bogus self-employed

Being a self-employed haulier does not mean working like an employee without an employment contract.

You can be genuinely self-employed even if you work for a single client. In transport there is even the TRADE status (economically dependent self-employed worker) when at least 75% of your income comes from one client and you meet the other legal requirements.

For your activity to be genuinely self-employed, you must keep the ability to organise your work and take on the business risk.

Signs of genuine self-employment

  • Your own vehicle or one legally at your disposal.
  • A transport licence in your name or properly linked to your business.
  • Your own invoicing.
  • A written commercial contract.
  • The ability to organise your work.
  • Covering fuel, maintenance and other costs.
  • Pay linked to the service or result.
  • No direct employer-style control over hours and holidays.

Signs of bogus self-employment

  • The company sets your hours as if you were an employee.
  • You only use a vehicle controlled by the company.
  • You have no transport licence of your own and no ability to take on contracts.
  • You are paid a fixed amount similar to a salary.
  • The company decides routes, breaks and holidays.
  • You work integrated with, and indistinguishable from, the company's staff.
  • You take on no financial risk.

Law 20/2007, the Self-Employed Workers' Statute, regulates self-employed activity and sets out the conditions for TRADE status. Pay particular attention to article 11 and the eleventh additional provision, which covers the transport sector.

Checklist before accepting each job

Before you hit the road, check:

  • CAP: valid and appropriate for your activity.
  • Licence: category suitable for the vehicle.
  • Transport licence: authorisation available.
  • Insurance: policy in force.
  • ITV: valid date.
  • Vehicle: maintenance and safety equipment checked.
  • Control document: job details filled in correctly.
  • Goods: weight, nature and conditions known.
  • Route: restrictions, tolls and access zones checked.
  • Filing: paperwork saved for future checks.

Frequently asked questions about becoming a self-employed haulier

Can I be a self-employed haulier with a van?

Yes, but you must check the van's GVW, the type of service and whether you need a transport authorisation. For international haulage with vehicles over 2.5 tonnes, additional requirements may apply.

Does the CAP replace the transport licence?

No. The CAP proves the driver's professional qualification. The transport licence authorises the road haulage activity. They are different obligations.

Can I work for a single company and still be self-employed?

Yes, as long as there is genuine self-employed activity, you have your own means, you take on business risk and you meet the TRADE conditions where applicable.

What documents should I carry before each job?

At a minimum, check your CAP, driving licence, transport licence, insurance, ITV and the job paperwork. Depending on the goods and the route, additional documents may be needed.
