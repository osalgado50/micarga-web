The transport to come

The sustainable future of freight transport: decarbonisation, eco-incentives and multimodality

Sustainable freight transport is no longer a trend. It is an economic, regulatory and operational requirement.

From now on, you will have to move goods with fewer emissions, make better use of resources and show more efficient management of every haul. Road transport will remain essential, but it will coexist with rail, shipping, electrification and digital logistics documentation.

The transition is already under way. And the companies that adapt first will be able to cut costs, access funding and compete more strongly.

Decarbonisation will shape freight transport through to

2030

The European Union has set concrete targets for cutting transport emissions. The overall goal is to reduce net greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55 % by 2030 compared with 1990.

Freight transport will have to contribute to that change across all its modes:

  • Road: electric trucks, alternative fuels and more efficient routes.
  • Rail: greater capacity to carry loads over long distances.
  • Maritime: fleet renewal and the use of low-emission fuels.
  • Logistics: fewer empty kilometres, more planning and digital documents.

Sustainability is not just about buying a new vehicle. It also means making better use of every trip, reducing waiting times, avoiding administrative errors and coordinating everyone in the chain.

Start by reviewing your routes, your fuel consumption and your paperwork. Every operational improvement cuts costs and prepares your company for the new requirements.

250 million euros to renew the maritime fleet

Shipping carries an essential share of world trade. That is why its decarbonisation will be one of the top priorities of the coming years.

Spain has planned 250 million euros between 2026 and 2030 to drive the renewal and modernisation of the maritime fleet. The programme includes:

  • 200 million euros to renew the merchant fleet.
  • 150 million euros to build new low-emission vessels.
  • 50 million euros to convert and modernise existing vessels.
  • 49.61 million euros earmarked for low-emission technologies and fuels.

The goal is to cut greenhouse gas emissions from shipping by at least 20 % by 2030 compared with 2008, with the ambition of reaching 25 %.

These investments will bring in more efficient vessels, adapt engines and use alternative fuels such as renewable methanol, hydrogen, green ammonia or renewable fuels of non-biological origin.

The information on the Spanish maritime decarbonisation plan shows where the sector is heading.

Eco-incentives make modal shift more competitive

Moving part of the freight from road to rail or sea can cut emissions, but it also means adapting operations, contracts and routes.

To make that shift easier, the Ministry of Transport has launched eco-incentive programmes for sustainable transport under the Recovery, Transformation and Resilience Plan. In the third call of the maritime eco-incentive, more than 11.8 million euros were awarded to 32 freight transport companies, shippers and logistics operators. These companies moved trailers, semi-trailers or rigid heavy vehicles by ship instead of covering the whole journey by road.

There is also a rail scheme. In its second call, 21.8 million euros were awarded to seven private rail freight operators.

These grants pursue three concrete benefits:

  • Fewer emissions: fewer kilometres are driven solely by road.
  • Less congestion: the main transport routes are decongested.
  • More competitiveness: part of the initial cost of switching models is offset.

The joint maritime and rail eco-incentive programme has an initial budget of 120 million euros, financed with European NextGenerationEU funds.

Check the available programmes on the Ministry of Transport's electronic office and see whether your business could benefit.

Don't wait for modal shift to become mandatory. Work out now which journeys you can combine with rail or sea.

Multimodality will be the key to more efficient transport

Multimodality means using several modes of transport within a single logistics operation.

For example:

1. The truck collects the goods at the point of origin. 2. The train covers the main long-distance leg. 3. Shipping connects ports and international corridors. 4. Another truck completes the final delivery.

This system lets you assign each leg to the most efficient mode. The truck keeps its essential role in the first and last mile, while rail and ship can take on long routes with less impact per tonne carried.

For it to work, you need to coordinate:

  • Loading and unloading times.
  • Train or ship bookings.
  • Customs and transport documents.
  • Tracking of the goods.
  • Signatures and proofs of delivery.
  • Communication between shipper, carrier and operator.

This is where digitalisation is decisive. If each participant works with different documents, errors, calls and delays multiply. If everyone uses up-to-date information, the operation is faster and traceable.

Electrification will change road transport

The electric truck will play an increasingly important role, especially in urban distribution, regional routes and runs that regularly return to base.

Its main advantages are:

  • Lower energy consumption per kilometre.
  • Zero direct emissions while driving.
  • Less noise in urban areas.
  • Lower maintenance costs.
  • Easier access to low-emission zones.

However, electrifying heavy transport takes planning. You will have to look at range, charging times, available power and where the chargers are located.

For long-distance routes, solutions based on hydrogen, advanced biofuels and other technologies will also be developed. There will be no single solution for every company. The right option will depend on the type of load, the distance and how often the trips are made.

European regulation on heavy-duty vehicles sets CO₂ emission reduction targets of 45 % by 2030, 65 % by 2035 and 90 % from 2040, using 2019 levels as the baseline.

Read the European framework on truck and bus emissions and start planning the renewal of your fleet.

The sustainable future starts with concrete decisions

Sustainable freight transport will be more digital, multimodal and efficient. Maritime decarbonisation will receive major investment. Eco-incentives will boost rail and sea. Electric trucks will grow on the right routes. And companies will need tighter control of their data and documents.

You don't need to transform your whole operation in a single day.

Start with simple actions:

  • Review which routes can combine road and rail.
  • Consider whether shipping could replace any journey.
  • Work out the real cost of renewing part of your fleet.
  • Check the grants available.
  • Digitalise your control documents.
  • Cut paper use and avoid duplicating information.
  • Measure your kilometres, consumption and waiting times.

Sustainability is also efficiency. Fewer errors, less waiting, fewer repeated documents and more control over every operation.
