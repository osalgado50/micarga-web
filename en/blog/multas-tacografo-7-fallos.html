Tachograph fines: the 7 mistakes that will cost you most, and how to avoid them

One slip can turn an ordinary job into a penalty of hundreds or thousands of euros.

Regulation (EC) 561/2006 governs driving and rest times; Regulation (EU) 165/2014 governs tachograph use. If you need to go over the exact limits, they are in driving and rest times: the complete guide — here we focus on the 7 mistakes that lead to most penalties.

1. Driving without inserting the card properly

Driving with no card, with someone else's, or without checking that the tachograph has recognised it is one of the most basic mistakes and one of the most troublesome. Do it before you start: insert the card, check the details on the display and confirm the mode switches to driving when you set off.

2. Not making manual entries

The card doesn't record what you do away from the tachograph. Gaps with no accounted-for activity are a red flag at an inspection. Fill them in there and then, don't leave it for later.

3. Exceeding the maximum driving time

Traffic, delays at loading or a demanding customer don't justify pushing the minutes. Plan the stop before you reach the limit and use routes with rest areas you know.

4. Cutting rest periods or taking them wrongly

Finishing late and starting early, confusing availability with rest, or reducing the weekly rest without planning the compensation are the most repeated mistakes. A reduction of more than 20% can be a serious offence; over 50%, a very serious one.

5. Tampering with the tachograph or the records

The costliest and most dangerous item on the list. Magnets, altered software or manipulated cards can bring very high fines, the vehicle being held and loss of the company's good repute. If the unit fails, go to an approved workshop — never tamper with it.

6. Downloading card or vehicle data late

The driver card must be downloaded at least every 28 days; the vehicle unit every 90. Set up a calendar with reminders before the deadline and keep the files backed up.

7. Driving with a non-compliant or uncalibrated tachograph

Check the date of the last calibration and the approved workshop that carried it out. From 1 July 2026 this also applies to certain vehicles over 2.5 tonnes on international journeys or cabotage.

Frequently asked questions

How much can a tachograph fine be?

It depends on the offence: minor, serious or very serious, with indicative bands of €301-400, €1,501-2,000 and €3,301-4,600. Tampering can carry further consequences.

Who gets the penalty: the driver or the company?

It depends on where responsibility is established: the driver for breaches while driving, the company for how the work is organised or the vehicle maintained.

What if the tachograph fails during a journey?

Make the manual records needed, keep evidence of the fault and go to an approved workshop. Don't tamper with it.

Does Mi Carga replace the tachograph?

No. It helps you produce and organise transport paperwork; it does not change or replace the tachograph.
