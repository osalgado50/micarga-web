7 mistakes with your lorry's paperwork that can cost you a fine

Check them before an officer at the roadside does it for you.

An inspection can start with a simple question: "can I see your paperwork?". If a document is missing, has the wrong details or can't be shown, the journey can end in a penalty, lost time or the vehicle being held.

From 5 October 2026 the control document must be digital (the DeCA). If you need the full background to the rule, start with what the DeCA is and who it applies to.

1. Setting off with no consignment note or control document

A delivery note, a loading order or an email is no substitute for a valid control document for the job. Check that there is a document for every load, that it travels with the vehicle from the start and that it matches the actual journey.

2. Incomplete or incorrect details

Having a PDF is not enough if the tax number, the registration plate, the origin, the destination or the weight are wrong. Always check loader, carrier, vehicle, goods, quantity and date before setting off.

3. Sticking with paper when the digital DeCA applies

From 5 October 2026, a paper delivery note or control document is no longer valid for transport covered by the obligation. The DeCA must be a native PDF, with a QR code, traceability and at least a year's retention.

4. Paperwork that has expired or doesn't match the vehicle

Roadworthiness test, insurance, driver CPC, transport card and ADR certificates all expire on different dates. Build a fixed check before every journey instead of looking at it once a year.

5. Not getting the ADR paperwork right

If you carry dangerous goods, an error in the UN number or the proper shipping name can make the offence a more serious one. The full detail is in which ADR documents the Guardia Civil can ask you for.

6. Not being able to show the document at the check

A DeCA sitting in an old email or behind a link that asks for a password is no use during a check: the URL has to work and download the PDF directly, with the QR visible on the driver's copy.

7. Altering or falsifying the transport details

Changing a registration plate, shaving off weight or altering the origin is not a simple correction: if the details don't match reality, falsification can be a very serious offence. If something needs correcting, produce a new version of the document and keep the change history.
