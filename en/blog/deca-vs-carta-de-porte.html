DeCA vs consignment note: which document do you need for each load, and when?

Administrative or contractual, domestic or international: the difference explained with examples.

Not sure whether you need a DeCA, a CMR consignment note or both? It's a common muddle. The answer depends on three things: where the journey starts and ends, whether it is domestic or international, and whether you are carrying your own goods or someone else's.

From 5 October 2026, domestic haulage for hire and reward must use the digital DeCA. The CMR remains the reference document for international journeys.

The key difference: administrative versus contractual

The DeCA records the main details of a domestic haulage job for hire and reward: loader, actual carrier, origin and destination, goods and vehicle. Its purpose is to make administrative control and roadside inspection possible.

The CMR consignment note is the contractual document for international road haulage, under the 1956 Geneva CMR Convention. It works as evidence of the contract: who sends, who carries, who receives, on what terms and with what reservations or incidents.

When do you need a DeCA?

When a job for hire and reward starts and ends within Spain — say you load in Madrid and deliver in Valencia — or when it is cabotage carried out entirely on Spanish territory, even if the carrier is foreign.

There are exceptions, such as certain vehicles with a maximum authorised mass of 2 tonnes or less: always check the type of operation before ruling the obligation out.

When do you need a CMR consignment note?

When the place of loading and the place of delivery are in different countries: Spain–France, Spain–Portugal, or between any two other European countries. An EU internal border does not make the job a domestic one.

Do you ever need both at once?

On most international journeys crossing Spain, the CMR is enough. But if an operation has a separate domestic leg — say one carrier takes the load from Seville to Irún and another takes it on into France — the first leg may need its own DeCA and the second its CMR.

And if you carry dangerous goods?

Neither the CMR nor the DeCA replaces ADR paperwork. If your load is dangerous goods, also check which ADR documents the Guardia Civil can ask you for.
