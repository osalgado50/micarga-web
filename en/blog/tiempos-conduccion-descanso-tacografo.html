Driving and rest times: the guide to not fining yourself with your own tachograph

Regulation (EC) 561/2006 and Regulation (EU) 165/2014, explained with practical examples.

The tachograph records what you do and can be your best ally or the evidence of an offence. Driving well is not enough: you have to respect the times, select the right activity and plan every break and rest.

The figures to commit to memory

  • 4 hrs 30 min: maximum driving before a break.
  • 45 minutes: the compulsory break after those 4 hrs 30 min (it can be split into 15 + 30).
  • 9 hours: normal daily driving (extendable to 10 hours, twice a week at most).
  • 56 hours: maximum driving in one week.
  • 90 hours: maximum over 2 consecutive weeks.
  • 11 hours: normal daily rest (reducible to 9, up to 3 times between two weekly rests).
  • 45 hours: normal weekly rest (reducible to 24, with later compensation).

If you go over a limit, the tachograph records it. You can't erase the activity or explain it away with traffic or a customer in a hurry. Plan with room to spare: driving to the last minute is the quickest way to breach the rules.

The 45-minute break: when and how

It can be split into two parts: 15 minutes first, then 30. The order matters — the other way round doesn't count — and both parts must fall within the relevant driving period. Find the rest area before you use up the 4 hrs 30 min, leaving room for traffic or slip roads.

The most common mistake: confusing work with driving

Loading, unloading or waiting is not the same as driving, and it doesn't give you more hours at the wheel. The tachograph distinguishes driving, other work, availability and break/rest: select each activity correctly, because getting it wrong can change the calculation.

The weekly limit and the two-week one

Even if you keep to 9 hours a day, you can still breach the weekly limit (56 hrs) or the two-week total (90 hrs). For example: 54 hours in week 1 and 36 in week 2 already uses up the combined maximum. Always check the current week's total, the previous week's, and the 10-hour extensions already used.

Daily rest: don't leave it to the last moment

Normal rest is 11 consecutive hours (or 9 reduced hours, limited to 3 times between weekly rests). Look for parking before you reach the limit: if you take a while to find a space, you can delay the actual start of the rest without realising.

Weekly rest: 45 hours, or a compensated reduction

The normal weekly rest is 45 consecutive hours, which cannot be taken in the cab. You can reduce it to 24 hours, but you must make up the difference in another rest of at least 9 hours within the set period, and the weekly rest must start before 6 periods of 24 hours have elapsed since the previous one.

From 1 July 2026, the rules also apply to certain vehicles over 2.5 tonnes on international journeys or cabotage. If you run heavy vans on that kind of work, check whether you fall within scope.
