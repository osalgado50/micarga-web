International and multimodal

International road freight: the documents, the rules and the details you need to master

International road freight takes far more than loading, driving and delivering. You have to keep on top of documents, borders, driving times, insurance, cabotage and whatever the destination country asks for.

A mistake in the freight paperwork can mean delays, an immobilised vehicle, fines or trouble getting paid for the job.

The fix is simple: get all the paperwork ready before you set off and carry it on your phone.

Avoid trouble at the border with the right paperwork

Every international run can call for different documents. Driving between Spain and France is not the same as taking goods between Spain and the United Kingdom, Switzerland or Morocco.

Before you start the route, check these documents:

  • CMR consignment note: it evidences the international road transport contract, identifies the goods and sets out who is responsible for what.
  • Commercial invoice: needed to justify the value of the goods, especially where there is customs clearance.
  • Packing list: sets out packages, weights, measurements and what the load contains.
  • Customs paperwork: needed when you cross an external border of the European Union.
  • EORI number: identifies the company to the customs authorities.
  • T1 or T2 transit documents: may be needed depending on the origin, the destination and the customs procedure for the goods.
  • Special authorisations: required for dangerous goods, oversized loads, livestock or products subject to specific controls.
  • Insurance and vehicle documents: have them to hand for any inspection.
  • Driver's documents: driving licence, CPC qualification card, passport or ID and, where applicable, a posting certificate.

What it means in practice: carrying the right document stops the route being halted over an incomplete invoice, a wrong detail or an authorisation that is not in the file.

Use the CMR consignment note as your main document

The CMR consignment note is the basic document of international road freight. It is used when the origin and the destination are in different countries and the goods travel by road.

It must include, among other things:

  • Name and address of the consignor.
  • Name and address of the carrier.
  • Place and date of loading.
  • Intended place of delivery.
  • Description of the goods.
  • Number of packages and type of packaging.
  • Gross weight or quantity.
  • Registration of the vehicle and the trailer.
  • Customs instructions.
  • Any reservations or issues noted.
  • Signatures of consignor, carrier and consignee.

The CMR also serves as proof of delivery and helps settle claims for damage, loss or delay.

Do not confuse the CMR with a bill of lading

The bill of lading is the sea consignment note. It is used mainly in shipping and does a different job, such as evidencing receipt of the goods and, in certain cases, representing title to them.

On the road, the document to keep on top of is the CMR. If you combine road and sea, you may need both: a CMR for the road leg and a bill of lading for the sea leg.

Move to the e-CMR and get ready for digital transport

The e-CMR is the electronic version of the international consignment note. It lets you check the information from your phone, share it with consignors and consignees, and record the delivery without depending on several paper copies.

Digitisation is moving fast from 2026. Even so, you need to tell two obligations apart:

  • e-CMR: whether it is accepted depends on the applicable framework, the countries involved and the agreement between the parties.
  • Electronic DeCA: it will be compulsory in Spain from 5 October 2026 for domestic public road haulage and for the cabotage operations it applies to.

The e-CMR does not automatically replace every customs document or specific authorisation. Nor does it remove the need to check what each border requires.

Electronic DeCA: the key date is 5 October 2026

Sustainable Mobility Act 9/2025 makes the digitisation of the administrative control document compulsory.

From 5 October 2026, the DeCA must meet specific technical requirements, set out in Resolution BOE-A-2026-12784:

  • Native PDF: scanning a sheet of paper is not enough.
  • QR code: it must give access to the document.
  • Direct download: the URL cannot ask for registration, a password or intermediate steps.
  • Traceability: the dates of creation and modification must be on record.
  • Retention: the document must stay available for the required period.
  • Immutability: if any detail has to be corrected, you must create a new document.

On a purely international job, the main document is still the CMR or an accepted e-CMR. Cabotage carried out in Spain, on the other hand, can fall under the electronic DeCA as domestic transport.

Do not wait for the change. Set your system up now and don't let paper leave you out of compliance.

Check the cabotage rules before you take on a domestic job

Cabotage is domestic transport carried out within a country by a company established in another State.

Example: a Portuguese company delivers a load in Spain and then carries goods between Madrid and Valencia. That second job is cabotage.

The main European Union rules set limits on:

  • How many operations you may carry out.
  • How long you have after the international journey.
  • What paperwork you must carry.
  • Recording entries, loadings and deliveries.
  • A cooling-off period before doing cabotage again in the same country.

As a general guide, after an international operation you may carry out up to three cabotage operations in seven days, provided you meet the applicable conditions. After that there may be a four-day period before you can do cabotage again in the same State with the same vehicle.

Always check your specific situation. A mistake with dates, documents or records can turn a profitable job into a fine.

Fit your vehicle for the tachograph that becomes compulsory on 1 July 2026

From 1 July 2026, commercial vehicles over 2.5 tonnes and up to 3.5 tonnes doing international freight or cabotage must be fitted with a second-generation smart tachograph.

The obligation covers:

  • Fitting a Smart Tachograph 2.
  • Recording driving, breaks, rest and other periods of activity.
  • Keeping within the European driving limits.
  • Downloading and keeping the data.
  • Carrying the corresponding driver card.
  • Being ready for checks on earlier periods.

The usual limits are:

  • 9 hours' driving a day, which can go up to 10 hours twice a week.
  • 56 hours' driving a week.
  • 90 hours over two consecutive weeks.
  • A 45-minute break after 4 hours 30 minutes' driving.

If you use a 2.8 or 3.2 tonne van to cross borders, don't wait for the first inspection. Fit the equipment and sort out the records before you start the job.

Protect your business with the right insurance

International transport is normally governed by the CMR Convention. As a general rule, the carrier's liability is limited to 8.33 units of account per kilogram of goods lost or damaged, save in specific circumstances.

That does not mean every loss is automatically covered.

Check:

  • Carrier's liability insurance: it covers the liability arising from the contract.
  • Goods in transit insurance: it protects the real value of the load.
  • Exclusions: look at theft, temperature damage, inherent vice or poor packaging.
  • Excesses: know how much you will have to bear yourself.
  • Claim deadlines: act quickly if something goes wrong.
  • Reservations on delivery: record visible damage before you sign.

Photograph the goods, note the reservations on the CMR and keep the signatures. Evidence kept properly makes the claim easier and saves arguments later.

Checklist before you leave Spain

Before you set the lorry moving, check:

  • CMR complete and signed.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Customs paperwork, where applicable.
  • EORI and transit documents.
  • Special permits or ADR.
  • Insurance in force.
  • Driver's documents.
  • Tachograph working and card inserted.
  • Cabotage record, where applicable.
  • A digital copy on your phone.
  • Load details, weight and registration checked.

A minute spent checking can save hours waiting at the border.
