Load and securing

Securing the load: the rules everyone forgets until the lorry is taken off the road

A badly secured load can stop the job just as surely as incomplete paperwork.

The tachograph and ADR paperwork take up most of the attention on checklists, but Royal Decree 563/2017 allows roadside inspection of something just as decisive: how the load is secured. A serious deficiency can force you to put it right before going on; a dangerous one can end in the vehicle being held and a fine.

If you came looking for driving limits or the ADR checklist, they are in driving and rest times and in ADR paperwork — here we deal only with load securing.

What the inspection covers

The aim is to check that the goods do not move while the vehicle is running, cannot fall outside the load space, do not affect driving, do not endanger other vehicles and stay within axle weight limits. Annex III of the Royal Decree refers to technical standards such as EN 12195-1, on calculating lashing forces.

The most common securing failures

  • Damaged straps: cuts, wear or an illegible label, so they no longer provide the strength required.
  • An unblocked load: gaps between pallets and walls that let the goods shift under braking.
  • Not enough lashing points: several straps are no use if they are not anchored to suitable points.
  • No edge protection: the edges of the load can cut the straps and make them less effective.
  • Badly distributed weight: the total mass can be correct and still exceed the maximum load on one axle.
  • Damaged doors or side panels where these are used to block the load.

A worked example

You carry eight pallets and leave a 20-centimetre gap between the load and the front wall, with only two straps and no edge protectors. Under braking, the pallets can move forward and damage the wall or shift towards the cab. The problem is not only the fine: you can also cause an accident and lose the load.

Checklist before setting off

  • Place the load as close to the front wall as you safely can.
  • Fill the gaps with suitable blocking systems.
  • Use straps, bars, anti-slip mats and edge protectors.
  • Check the tension and condition of every component.
  • Check how the weight is distributed across the axles.
  • Take a photograph of the finished load, in case you need to account for it later.
If your load is dangerous goods, securing it does not replace ADR marking or equipment — also check vehicle, marking and ADR routes.
