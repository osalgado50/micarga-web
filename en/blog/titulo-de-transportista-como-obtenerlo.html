Setting up and running the business

Haulier qualification: what it is, how to get it and what it lets you do

If you want to work legally as a haulier, you need to be clear on two concepts: the haulier qualification and the transport licence.

They are not the same thing.

The qualification proves your professional competence. The licence is the administrative authorisation that allows your company or business to provide certain road transport services.

In this article you will see what you need, how to get it, which subjects are in the exam and how it relates to good repute, the transport manager and the transport authorisation.

What is the haulier qualification?

The so-called haulier qualification is the everyday name for the certificate of professional competence for road transport.

This certificate shows that you have the knowledge needed to run a goods or passenger transport business.

Professional competence is one of the requirements for entering the haulage profession, along with:

  • Good repute.
  • Financial standing.
  • Effective establishment in Spain.
  • Compliance with tax and social security obligations.

These requirements come, among other rules, from Regulation (EC) 1071/2009, which sets the common conditions for working as a road haulier in the European Union.

What does this qualification get you?

With the certificate of professional competence you can:

  • Prove the qualification needed to work as a haulier.
  • Be appointed as a company's transport manager.
  • Meet the professional competence requirement to apply for an authorisation.
  • Legally run a goods or passenger transport business.
  • Apply for the transport licence, as long as you also meet the other requirements.

The qualification does not replace the authorisation. It is an essential part of the application, but you will also have to prove financial standing, good repute and the other required conditions.

First prove your competence. Then apply for the transport licence.

Who needs the haulier qualification?

You need to prove professional competence when you want to carry out a public road transport activity that falls within the scope of administrative authorisation.

In public goods transport, the requirement is especially relevant in these cases:

  • Vehicles over 3,500 kg MMA: you need to meet the requirements for entering the profession and hold the corresponding authorisation.
  • International transport with vehicles over 2,500 kg MMA: the requirements for access to the international market may also apply.
  • Transport companies: they must have a person who provides the professional competence and effectively runs the business.

Certain light domestic transport may have specific rules depending on the maximum authorised mass, the type of service and the applicable authorisation. So don't confuse driving a van with legally operating as a public transport company.

Also, the qualification is not the same as:

  • The driving licence.
  • The CAP.
  • The tachograph driver card.
  • The transport licence.

Each document has a different purpose. The driving licence lets you drive. The CAP proves your qualification as a professional driver. The haulier qualification proves your competence to run a transport business.

Haulier qualification and transport licence: what's the difference?

The difference is simple:

  • Haulier qualification: proves professional competence. It shows that you can run a transport business.
  • Transport licence: an administrative authorisation. It allows you to provide transport services with authorised vehicles.
  • Driver CAP: proves professional qualification. It allows you to drive certain vehicles professionally.
  • Driving licence: proves fitness to drive. It entitles you to drive a specific category.

To apply for a transport licence, the company must meet the conditions required by the Administration. These include professional competence, which can be provided in two ways:

  • With your own certificate, if you have passed the exam or hold a valid qualification.
  • Through a transport manager, hired by or legally linked to the company.

The transport manager is not a token figure. They must effectively and continuously run the transport activities. Their duties can include managing maintenance, reviewing contracts and documents, organising loads and vehicles, basic accounting and checking safety procedures.

Check the official requirements for the transport manager before submitting your application.

How to get the haulier qualification

You have two main routes to prove professional competence.

1. Pass the official exam

The most common option is to sit the exam called by the autonomous community where you usually live or where you meet the administrative conditions of the call.

As a general rule, you need to hold one of these qualifications:

  • Bachillerato (upper secondary education) or equivalent.
  • Técnico qualification, from intermediate-level Vocational Training.
  • Técnico Superior qualification, from higher-level Vocational Training.
  • University bachelor's or postgraduate degree.

You will also have to:

  • Submit the application within the deadline.
  • Pay the corresponding fee.
  • Prove your residence or registration on the local census, as set out in the call.
  • Use a digital certificate, electronic DNI or another identification system if the procedure is online.

The specific conditions may vary between autonomous communities. Always check the current call in your region. For example, some administrations publish the information through their transport procedures portals, such as the Generalitat de Catalunya or the Generalitat Valenciana.

2. Prove a specific qualification

The Técnico Superior en Transporte y Logística qualification may allow you to obtain the certificate of professional competence directly, without sitting the specific exam, under the applicable administrative conditions.

Don't assume any private course is valid. The training must correspond to an officially recognised qualification or route.

Before enrolling, check which document you will receive and whether it can be used to prove professional competence.

What is the exam like?

The professional competence exam is organised into two tests:

  • Multiple-choice test: questions with several possible answers.
  • Practical test: case studies based on the real management of a transport company.

The usual format includes:

  • Up to 200 multiple-choice questions in the first part.
  • Case studies in the second part.
  • About 2 hours per test.
  • Penalties for wrong answers, depending on the call.
  • A minimum score in each test and an overall score to pass the exam.

The exact criteria may be set out in the regional call. Don't study just to memorise questions. The aim is to show that you can run a company, calculate costs, interpret documents and make the right decisions.

Subjects in the professional competence qualification

The official syllabus covers the areas you will need to handle day to day in a transport company:

  • Civil law: contracts, liabilities, claims and damage to goods.
  • Commercial law: businesses, companies, obligations and how a business operates.
  • Social law: employment contracts, Social Security, working hours and rest periods.
  • Tax law: VAT, taxes, tax obligations and vehicle-related taxes.
  • Financial management: fixed and variable costs, budgets, profitability, insurance and cash flow.
  • Market access: authorisations, transport licences, permits, documents and cabotage.
  • Technical operations: weights, dimensions, maintenance, loading, stowage and dangerous goods.
  • Road safety: drivers, vehicles, accident prevention and safety procedures.

These subjects are not isolated theory. They help you avoid mistakes that can lead to fines, lost profitability, vehicles being immobilised or problems with your authorisation.

For example, knowing your cost per kilometre lets you calculate a profitable rate. Mastering the paperwork stops an inspection from bringing your service to a halt. Understanding stowage reduces the risk of damage and claims.

Good repute: the requirement you must protect

Professional competence is not enough if you lose your good repute.

Good repute relates to the record of the company, its owner and the transport manager. Certain convictions or serious infringements can affect the ability to carry on the business.

The areas that can have an impact include:

  • Driving and rest times.
  • Tachograph use.
  • Road safety.
  • Transport of dangerous goods.
  • Overloading.
  • Market access.
  • Driving licences.
  • Working conditions.
  • Roadworthiness tests.
  • International transport.

A serious infringement can mean more than a fine. In some cases it can affect the authorisation, the transport manager or the future of the company.

Does the haulier qualification expire?

The certificate of professional competence does not work like the CAP, which requires periodic renewal. In general, the qualification has no ordinary expiry date.

However, you must keep meeting the other conditions to operate:

  • Keep your good repute.
  • Meet the financial standing requirement.
  • Keep the company properly established.
  • Have a valid transport manager.
  • Meet your tax and social security obligations.
  • Keep the transport authorisation active and in order.

In addition, European rules provide for refreshing your knowledge in certain cases, especially when a holder of the certificate has not continuously run a company for a long period.

Passing once is not enough. You have to keep complying at all times.

Checklist for applying for your transport licence

Before starting your business, check these points:

  • Professional competence: you hold the qualification or have a transport manager.
  • Good repute: there is no circumstance that prevents you from operating.
  • Financial standing: you can show the required resources.
  • Establishment: you have a base and documentation accessible to the Administration.
  • Vehicle: it meets the technical, tax and administrative conditions.
  • Tax Agency: you are registered for the corresponding activity.
  • Social Security: you meet your obligations as a self-employed person or company.
  • Documentation: you can provide contracts, certificates and supporting documents.
  • Authorisation: you have applied for the right licence for your activity.

Don't start transporting for third parties without first checking your administrative situation. A seemingly simple job can turn into a fine if you don't have the right authorisation.
