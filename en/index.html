From 5 October 2026, paper is no longer valid

The Sustainable Mobility Act 9/2025 and the update to the ROTT make digital administrative control documents mandatory in Spain. Avoid roadside penalties by making the change today.

  • QR code for inspections: Show the QR on your phone. The officer (Guardia Civil) scans it and validates the digital control document there and then.
  • Legal traceability: The app keeps a tamper-proof record of when each consignment note was created, and of every change made to it.
  • Mandatory 12-month retention: All your PDFs are stored securely in the cloud, as the law requires.

We meet the Resolution of 5 June 2026, point by point

The rule that implements the DeCA (BOE-A-2026-12784) sets very specific technical requirements. Here is what it demands and how Mi Carga meets it:

  • Natively digital PDF, 5 MB maximum (sections 2.1 and 2.2) — we build the document from the data, never from a scan. Our PDFs weigh around 0.5 MB.
  • Creation and modification timestamps as metadata (section 2.1) — recorded in the file itself and in the audit trail.
  • QR code with the document's unique URL (section 2.3) — printed on every PDF.
  • The URL downloads the PDF directly (sections 3.3 and 3.4) — scanning the QR downloads the file: no landing pages, no passwords, no buttons, all of which the rule expressly rules out.
  • HTTPS with TLS 1.2 or above (section 3.1) — all traffic is encrypted.
  • A repository available for inspection (section 1.3) — each document is stored as a file, not just as data.
  • Kept for at least a year (section 2.4) — your documents stay downloadable after the job is done.
  • A copy for the driver before the job starts (section 7) — on the phone or printed, always with its QR.

The Ministry provides no official app for issuing the DeCA: the rule sets the requirements that each company's own software must meet. Mi Carga is that software.

Penalties and the 2026 LOTT scale: what is at stake?

From 5 October 2026, presenting the document on paper or as a photo or scan counts as not carrying it. The Spanish Land Transport Act (LOTT) treats breaches with the utmost severity:

  • Fines of €401 to €600 (Serious offence, Art. 141.17 LOTT) for not carrying the electronic control document, carrying it with incomplete data or in a paper format that is no longer accepted. There is a 30% discount if it is paid within the following 30 days.
  • Fines of up to €6,000 (very serious offence) e the lorry immobilised on the spot by the Guardia Civil in cases of falsified data, repeat offences, or refusal to give digital access at the roadside.
  • Fines of €4,001 for ADR transport for an incorrect consignment note or for exceeding the 1,000 points of the 1.1.3.6 exemption without the required equipment and plates.
  • Joint and shared liability: The penalty falls on the haulier who does the job and on the contracting shipper who booked it.
  • Risk to your transport licence: A build-up of very serious offences means losing the good-repute requirement the haulage company needs.
  • Fully covered with Mi Carga: A native PDF with an official QR code, generated in the cab in seconds, so the officer can check the load in five seconds with no argument.

What if my case is exempt? Not everybody needs the DeCA

The DeCA is only required for transport for hire or reward of goods, and only for domestic journeys (national transport and cabotage). Article 2 of Order FOM/2861/2012 also leaves a number of specific cases out. If yours is one of them, we will tell you: we would rather you did not pay us than have you pay for something you do not need.

  • Removals (art. 2.1.b). No small print: it is the clearest exemption of them all.
  • Parcel and last-mile delivery (art. 2.1.d), when the consignments are a small number of packages that one person can handle. That same courier does need a DeCA the day they carry pallets.
  • Own-account transport: if you carry your own goods in your own vehicles, you are not even within the scope of the rule. You do have to carry documents on board proving it (art. 102.2 LOTT), but they do not have to be electronic.
  • International transport: the documents of the international conventions apply, such as the CMR. Careful though: cabotage within Spain does require a DeCA.
  • Damaged or broken-down vehicles carried on special vehicles (art. 2.1.c), and lifting cranes that do not carry a load. A car transporter carrying new vehicles, on the other hand, does need a DeCA.
  • Funeral transport, cash and valuables, household waste, your own farm machinery and everything that does not require a transport authorisation (art. 2.1.a, which refers to art. 33.2 of the ROTT).

The threshold is not 3.5 tonnes. It is 2

It is the most widespread confusion in the industry. In transport for hire or reward, the authorisation — and with it the DeCA — is required from 2 tonnes of maximum authorised mass upwards (art. 33.2.d of the ROTT), or 2.5 t for N1 vehicles running on alternative fuels. The 3,5 t only set additional requirements for access to the occupation, and the 2.5 t people talk about in the industry come from the European regulation on the Community licence, which applies to international transport, not to the Spanish DeCA.

In short: a 3-tonne van doing transport for hire or reward does need a DeCA, even though many people believe it does not.

Being exempt from the transport authorisation always exempts you from the DeCA. The other way round it does not: a removals or parcel company needs its operator licence and even so issues no DeCA. And there are grey areas the rule has never pinned down — the «small number of packages», or the flatbed truck recovering a crashed vehicle. If that is your case, write to us and we will look at it with you before you risk it at a roadside check.
